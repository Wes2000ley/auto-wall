# Map Enhancer model research

Research checkpoint: 2026-10-26. Digital Art remains the installed baseline.
No tested candidate has demonstrated a better combination of clean surfaces,
readable fine lines and preserved small shapes on the inspected map crops.
This is an open search, not a claim that no better model exists.

The user wants a clean enlargement that retains the image's detail. More grain,
stronger contrast, sharper outlines or invented texture do not meet that target.
Real-CUGAN denoise 1–3 was also judged by the user: useful cleanup, but below
Digital Art. That feedback remains part of the selection criteria.

## Evidence and reproduction

[model-trial-results.json](model-trial-results.json) contains sanitized numerical
results, source-image hashes, crop coordinates, prepared graph hashes and output
digests. [anime-trials.json](../tools/anime-trials.json) pins the source weights,
download locations, input contracts and reported licenses.
[README-model-trials.md](README-model-trials.md) explains how to reproduce a trial.
Original maps, model weights and generated images remain in private development
directories. None are bundled with these research tools.

The catalog contains 61 model/strength variants: 60 passed native checks and
completed browser comparisons on five crops each (300 candidate comparisons),
plus 50 repeated baseline comparisons across ten batches. One variant remains
a native preparation failure. All 1,090 saved candidate PNGs were decoded and
checked for expected dimensions; their digests are recorded in the results file.

The browser comparison uses the module's bundled ONNX Runtime 1.30.0 and tile
helpers, requesting WebGPU explicitly. Tests ran on Windows, Chrome 156 and an
RTX 3080. Native validation used Python 3.13.1, PyTorch 2.11, Spandrel 0.4.2 and
ONNX Runtime 1.25.1 CPU. This does not test another machine or prove that every
operator executed on the GPU.

The five original 1536×1024 maps are Emberroot Burrow, Frostwake Beacon,
Ashenwatch Hold, Coppercap Warren and Blackbriar Kilns. Each comparison measures
one 160×128 interior region with 48 real source pixels of context on each side.
The regions cover paper drawings, masonry, rugs, wood and foliage. The purple
door and gray architectural markings visible in some outputs already exist in
the source images. They are not labels or overlays generated by the harness.
These fixtures and outputs are not corrected training data.

Every completed candidate gets five crop comparisons, saved at 2× and 4× as
lossless PNGs. A model runs once at its native scale, followed by the same
browser high-quality resize. A 1× cleanup network does not become a learned
2×/4× upscaler merely because its result is resized. No post-sharpening,
bilateral smoothing or lossy WebP compression is used in this comparison.

This is screening on small crops from a related art-style corpus. There is no
matched high-resolution ground truth, objective quality ranking or full-map
quality validation. All completed outputs were produced for all five maps;
manual visual inspection varied by batch, as recorded below. A lack of a win
on inspected examples does not establish failure on every map style.

The results file records individual model timings and compatibility diagnostics.
Times are medians of one warmed crop measurement per map, including pixel
assembly/readback, excluding session creation, reference validation, whole-crop
comparison, encoding and saving. They are not controlled throughput benchmarks
or predictions of whole-map duration.

`seamError` in the private browser reports measures tiled versus whole-crop RGB
difference across the entire native-scale crop. Global pooling/attention can
change a tile throughout its area. It is not just a seam measurement and is not
a perceptual quality score. Adore and several CUGAN/Caelum variants have material
context dependence. They require a suitable tiling strategy before promotion,
even if a whole-crop example looks good. The first eleven-model batch did not
retain whole-crop PNGs; its tiled/whole numerical diagnostic was still measured.
An additional review of whole-crop Digital Art, CUGAN Pro 2×, CUNet noise3,
Waifaux NL3, Adore and Saryn covered all five maps at 2× and Frostwake at 4×.
The cleanup/detail tradeoffs persist without internal tile boundaries. This
does not validate those candidates on an entire map.

## Validation failures and repairs

Native preparation compares raw, unclipped output against an independent
PyTorch reference where supported, otherwise against the original author ONNX
graph. The latter is a weaker check and is identified in each result. The maximum
absolute error gate stays at `1e-3`. Output shapes and finite values are checked
on three input sizes; the dynamic SwinIR mask gets two extra rectangular sizes.
Browser candidates then compare against native CPU reference PNGs: maximum RGB
error at most 3, mean at most 0.5 on a 0–255 scale.

For Saryn and Gauss, author checkpoints are available but unsupported by the
development environment's Spandrel version. Their original FP32 ONNX graphs were
used as references; no independent PyTorch parity claim is made. Cyte's author
ONNX graph also passes against its separately loaded PyTorch checkpoint. Model
license terms are distinct from architecture licenses: the author's Saryn/Gauss
weight READMEs specify CC BY-NC 4.0. These models remain private research trials.

Three early problems were export/test defects and were repaired before judging
image quality:

- Alchemy's exported DySample normalizer froze its width/height at 32. A guarded
  graph adapter now derives those dimensions at runtime. It matches the
  unchanged PyTorch reference within approximately `5.19e-6`.
- SwinIR's tracing froze a cached attention mask. A separate export copy now
  builds the same mask from runtime dimensions. Multiple rectangular sizes pass
  against the unchanged PyTorch reference.
- The image-only export wrapper initially restored its reference network to
  training mode. Keeping both wrapper and reference in evaluation mode fixed
  dropout/batch-normalization comparisons for the Illustration DENOISE models.

Those failed trials remain recorded privately. They are not evidence that the
corresponding model family is inherently unusable. AnimeSharp V3 also needs its
actual pixel-unshuffle input contract; it was retested with that contract.

Real-CUGAN Pro conservative is a different unresolved numerical case. The
original network produces extreme values on the deterministic 32×32 random
probe (approximately −274,164 to 112,758). Prepared/reference raw maximum error
is `7.03125`, above the unchanged `1e-3` gate. Increasing the minimum size did
not resolve it. Natural-image crop checks are much closer, but do not erase the
failed probe. It remains a preparation failure, not a browser failure or a
visually ranked candidate.

The full 56-entry native recheck passed 55 and failed only that conservative
variant. Later added candidates were validated separately. Exporter wrapper
changes altered graph names/hashes for seven earlier exports; `--validate-only`
rechecked the exact earlier graphs without replacing their bytes. Public results
refer to the graphs actually used by the browser. Older browser batches predate
in-worker SHA verification; their graph bytes were checked separately. Newer
batches verify the hash in the worker before model creation. An intentionally
incorrect hash was rejected by the browser with a model-hash error.

The first three models rejected by the user—AnimeSharp V4 RCAN, its Fast RCAN PU
variant and IllustrationJaNai V3 DETAIL DAT2—are separate early trials. Their
unfinished runtime integration was removed. The early DETAIL graph was checked
under an older `0.01` tolerance and its approximately `0.00368` error does not
pass today's `1e-3` gate. Do not count it among current native passes. Thirteen
early images truncated by text/base64 transport were invalid and discarded from
the evidence; current PNGs use direct binary POSTs to the local harness server.

## Native-only experiments

These screens are separate from browser-validated catalog comparisons:

- **Baikal LoopSR V2:** the author's pinned weight/code releases were inspected
  and run with four refinement loops in native CUDA FP32. Five crops with 96
  real context pixels were compared to native Digital Art using the same Pillow
  Lanczos final resize. Inspected Emberroot, Frostwake and Ashenwatch examples
  preserve more unwanted paper/stone grain. No ONNX export or browser support
  claim was made. Sources: [model](https://huggingface.co/SnJake/Baikal-Anime-Upscaler/tree/bff283bf3b013416ab1005f38abb309173e37373)
  and [architecture](https://github.com/SnJake/ComfyUI-Baikal-Anime/tree/0ea345be77e5a132b905e51bc0109d4024b38903).
- **Digital Art D4 self ensemble:** eight flip/rotation predictions were inverted
  and averaged in FP32 on all five crops. Inspected Emberroot and Frostwake
  outputs changed edges slightly without a clear fine-detail win.
- **Digital Art input sampling:** 1×, 1.25×, 1.5× and 2× input enlargement was
  tested on five crops with 96 real context pixels, using the NCNN-quantized
  stored weights and the same final Lanczos resize. Inspected Frostwake and
  Blackbriar results become softer as input enlargement increases.
- **SCUNet prefilters:** PSNR/GAN cleanup followed by ArtCNN/Digital Art was
  screened on two maps. Early comparisons used different final resize methods
  and only 48 source context pixels. They do not establish an improvement and
  are excluded from the browser comparison totals.
- **DitherDeleter prefilters:** 25% and 50% cleanup blends before ArtCNN/Digital
  Art were run on all five crops, with matched 96-pixel real context and final
  Lanczos resize. No intermediate 8-bit quantization was used. Inspected
  Frostwake and Blackbriar examples still trade detail for softness. This has
  not been validated as a browser chain or on whole maps.

The search should continue with models trained for faithful restoration and
controlled cleanup. New candidates must beat the baseline on the same source
features at the same display scale before being added to the installed module.
Better controlled source/target data would also be needed before claiming that
training a custom network improves this balance. The map corpus alone is not
ground-truth high-resolution supervision.
